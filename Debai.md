Since the 1960s, NASA has left hardware across the solar system--on the
Moon, on Mars, and in deep space. From rovers and instruments that
completed their science missions to probes still traveling away from
Earth this hardware varies widely in both its purpose and current level
of functionality. Your challenge is to tell the story of some or all of
this equipment that introduces school-age space enthusiasts to the
hardware and the science it made possible.

## Background

For over half a century, humanity has sent incredible machines to the
Moon, Mars, and other celestial bodies. When their missions end, probes,
rovers, landers, descent stages, and scientific instruments are left
behind, standing---or flying---silently as monuments to human
exploration. While space agencies know the histories of these machines,
school-age space enthusiasts often hear only about active, ongoing
missions. There is a real opportunity here to inspire the next
generation using the rich, untold histories of these off-world artifacts
and the groundbreaking science that paved the way for today's
achievements and tomorrow's exploration.

To respond to this challenge, participants will want to know what types
of equipment currently reside on the Moon, on Mars, and elsewhere---old
rovers, descent stages, scientific payloads---and the specific missions
they facilitated. The priority is to translate complex historical and
scientific data into an engaging narrative.

People looking to learn about past space missions often work through
dense technical websites or historical textbooks that lack the spark of
imagination and storytelling needed to capture a younger or more casual
audience's attention. Potential projects could include an interactive
map of the Moon with clickable "ghost rovers," a digital graphic novel
told from the perspective of a dormant Mars lander, or an audio podcast
series chronicling the final days of a deep space instrument. We
encourage you to think outside the box and develop your own creative
alternatives!

## Objectives

The goal of this challenge is to create an engaging, story-driven
educational tool or narrative that teaches school-age and casual space
enthusiasts about the hardware left on the Moon, Mars, and elsewhere.
Your project might bring these silent or active artifacts (back) to
life, explaining what they are, why they were originally sent into
space, and the scientific discoveries they made and continue to make.

Your final project can take many forms, and we welcome a variety of
formats and technologies. For example, you might develop an interactive
web-based experience using HTML, CSS, or JavaScript; prototype an
educational video game using a game engine of your choice; write and
illustrate a digital children's book; produce a short animated video;
design an interactive 3D timeline; or take another approach entirely!

## Potential Considerations

Regardless of the medium or technology you choose, a strong narrative
focus will serve your project well. Consider weaving real historical
facts and mission data into your story, and showing your audience how
these missions operated rather than listing facts about them. The aim is
to draw a clear, exciting connection between the engineering of the
hardware and the scientific knowledge humanity gained from it. By the
end of your story, your audience might walk away feeling a personal
connection to these forgotten robotic explorers and inspired by the
science they conducted. Accuracy is important throughout!

*You may (but are not required to) consider the following:*

-   What age group is your story designed for?
-   How accessible is the format you chose to use?
-   Do you want to focus on specific missions and pieces of equipment,
    or tell a broader story about NASA's exploration?
-   What perspective(s) do you want to highlight?
